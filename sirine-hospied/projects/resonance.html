The project

RESONANCE is a concept website for a fictional underground music festival, designed as a school exercise in mockups and wireframing. The theme was ours to choose, so I went for a three-day, punk-inspired festival with a rebellious identity. I designed the desktop and mobile versions of the site, which present the festival, its headliners and its ticket options.

The goal of the exercise was to practice building a bold, strong artistic identity. The challenge was to make the site feel chaotic and raw while keeping it clear enough for a young, loud audience to find the lineup and buy a ticket in a few seconds.

Design choices

Inspiration

The festival’s universe is inspired by characters from Arcane and League of Legends, whose punk, street-art look gave the project its attitude. I combined it with brutalist references: graffiti, raw materials and ripped paper. Torn paper edges split each section like posters ripped off a wall, and a crossed-out “O” in the logo works as a rebel mark that returns across the site. Together they make the site feel raw and underground while the layout stays clear.

Colors

The palette plays on extreme contrast: very dark and very bright colors, used to mimic a neon and graffiti effect. Electric blue, pink and purple glow against a black background like spray paint at night, which ties the site to the festival’s underground, after-dark mood. The brightest gradients are kept for the buttons and the ticket cards, so the most important actions are always the first thing the eye finds.

Typography

Titles are set in Punk Kid, a rough, hand-sprayed typeface that reads like graffiti tags and gig posters, so the headlines carry the festival’s attitude. Body text uses Stretch Pro, a wide, clean face that stays legible on a busy neon background. The contrast between the messy headlines and the clean text lets the page be wild and bold while staying readable.
